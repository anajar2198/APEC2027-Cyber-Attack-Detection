1. REAL-TIME TITLE AND SOME REAL-TIME BODY WORDING -- RESTORED
I previously removed this wording before benchmarking the selected checkpoint.
That was premature. The new CPU benchmark measures 1,000 batch-1 validation
windows after 50 warm-ups, with all six heads synchronized. Mean processing is
4.50 ms, 95th percentile 5.19 ms, and maximum 9.26 ms, including raw/difference
preprocessing, standardization and output decoding. At the author's approximately
7-Hz rate, stride 16 permits 2,285.71 ms between scheduled windows.
The original title is restored, and a runtime subsection supports computational
real-time feasibility on the measured CPU. Event-level alarm delay remains a
separate measurement. A 128-sample nominal duration of 18.29 s is not an observed
attack-to-alarm delay. Sources: results/rebalanced_runtime.json/.csv and the
benchmark script. No retraining occurred.

2. RANKING METRICS -- AVAILABLE METRICS NOW REPORTED
The original generic mention of PR-AUC was replaced by the metrics reported for
the selected model. The saved predictions already gave average precision
0.9936595 and ROC-AUC 0.9757865. The revised paper now reports both (0.9937 and
0.9758). Average precision is named explicitly; it is not relabeled as a
trapezoidal precision-recall area. These values were not missing calculations.
Source: results/rebalanced_experiment_metrics.json, metrics.attack.

3. TEST ACCURACY / STRONG GENERALIZATION -- CHANGED, NOT DISPROVED
The old approximately 92% test result is replaced by 93.46% on the selected
23,540-window cohort. The partition is called validation because it was used
for model/epoch selection. The split is random at the overlapping-window level,
with seed 42, rather than independent recordings. The scope clarification does
not calculate poorer performance on an unseen recording; that performance has
not been measured. Actual confusion counts determine the reported accuracy.

4. SCALABLE / HIGH EDGE SCALABILITY -- NARROWED TO THE TESTED SETUP
The original general scalability claims and the comparison table's High/Low

motors can be distinguished from aggregate PCC features. No ten-motor result was

RUNTIME LIMITS
The benchmark uses an Intel Core Ultra 9 285K CPU with four intra-operation
threads and TensorFlow 2.21, without a GPU. It times completed recorded windows,
not live EMU acquisition or networking. The first graph-tracing call was
186.32 ms and is recorded separately. Outputs match the saved predictions to
within 4.33e-6. A full event-level delay measurement needs a verified mapping
from window timestamps to source samples and every scheduled prediction in
chronological order. The random validation subset omits intervening windows.
